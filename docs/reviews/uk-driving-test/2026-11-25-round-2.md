## What is right
- Five structures, not five skins: a top bar, a side route, bottom tabs, a contents page and a road with stops. That is the hard part, and it is done.
- The keeper's round 1 was thorough: axe on 48 pages, light and dark, at two widths; quizzes work by keyboard and without scripts; no cookies and no outside requests.
- Every answer cites its rule, and the unofficial notice is on every page.

## Which should be home: I disagree with Course path
The audience is first-time learners on phones, and what they come back to do is practise. **Quiz first** puts "Start a quiz" on the first screen, with bottom tabs a phone user knows, so I recommend it as home. On a phone, Course path spends its whole first screen on a menu of the same five links that its cards repeat just below, and the path shows no "you are here", so it is a contents list drawn as a path. Make **Road map** the showpiece: it is the most memorable of the five and the one a learner would show a friend.

## Worst first
1. **The title appears twice on every first screen** (site name in the header, then the same words as the heading), and the notice band takes two lines. On a phone, about a third of the first screen says the same thing three times. Show the title once, and shorten the notice on phones to one line: "Unofficial: not the DVSA or GOV.UK".
2. **No design shows progress.** Saved results are already in the browser. Use them: Road map lights the stops you have passed, Course path marks "you are here", Quiz first shows a ring per topic, the book ticks chapters read. Progress is what brings a learner back.
3. **No imagery.** The world of the theory test has strong pictures of its own: road signs, road markings, a hazard developing ahead. Draw your own in SVG (triangles for warnings, circles for orders, rectangles for information), never copied from the Highway Code's artwork. One drawing per design, on its first screen:
   - Road map: real signs along the road at each stop;
   - Highway Code book: a sign plate on its cover;
   - Quiz first: the sign the first question is about;
   - Course path: road markings as the path;
   - Plain page: none, by design.
4. **Course path on a phone:** collapse the route to a single "Step 2 of 5: Learn the rules" line, open on tap.
5. **Highway Code book:** make sure nothing in it can be read as the official Highway Code or its crown-copyright design. Keep "An unofficial study companion" under the title, as now.

# sovereignwebsite.ai restyle, round 2: a.design's brief

For website.a, 25 November 2026, 15:20 UTC. Dinis's verdict on round 1 (designs 6 to 10), relayed by sites.a: "these are also mainly 5 variations of the same design ... basically this needs to look like a super professional services site". He also wants three prices, **£0, £50 and £500**, with "one of those pricing pages".

**He is right, and my round 1 review missed it.** I checked that each design had its own sections; I did not ask whether a reader would see five different sites at a glance. They would not: the same header, the same serif headline in the same place, the same two buttons, the same North Studio strip, on every first screen. From this round the standard has a new first check (below), and I apply it before anything else.

## The new first check: five different sites at a glance
Put the five first screens side by side, at 390 px and again at 1366 px, and blur your eyes. If two of them have the same shape (header, then headline top left, then buttons, then a strip), they are the same design. **Each must differ in layout and information architecture**: where the headline sits, what the first screen is made of, how the reader moves through the site. Colour and type can be shared; structure cannot.

## Five directions for a professional services site
The brief's tokens (ivory, ink navy, cobalt, the system serif) may carry across, or each direction may take its own; what must change is the structure.
1. **Consultancy**: a long page in the manner of a professional firm. First screen: a short statement left, a "Services" grid of six tiles right (desktop) or below (phone); then a process strip (Ask, Design, Build, Hand over) across the width; then the people-free proof (what you own, numbers you can check); then pricing.
2. **Product (SaaS)**: a centred hero with one sentence and one button, then a large product screenshot (a real /designs/ page or a real site we keep, framed as a browser); then three proof points in a row (no customer logos); then the three-tier pricing table on the home itself.
3. **Portfolio first (agency)**: the first screen is the work: a full-width grid of real sites we keep (screenshots, labelled by kind, owners' agreement via sites.a), with a one-line statement over it; the offer and prices come after the work.
4. **Case study (editorial)**: the home is one story, told like a magazine case study: "How a painter got a website she owns", with a pull quote, a before and after, the timeline and the numbers, then "Yours next" and the prices. Use a fictional, labelled subject (as North Studio), never a real person without consent.
5. **Product tour (how it works)**: a split screen: steps listed down the left (1 You ask, 2 Five designs, 3 You pick, 4 It is yours), and on the right the matching screen for the active step (CSS-only, radio inputs and :checked, as design 8 did); on a phone the steps stack, each with its screen under it.

## Every direction includes
- **A pricing page** with three tiers in columns (stacked cards on a phone), each with: the price, its name, who it is for, what is included (a short list with ticks and words), what it does not include, and its one button; then a FAQ of five or six questions (folding `details`), and the comparison table. The tiers are **£0, £50 and £500**. Take the wording of what each includes from what the site has already published (the earlier live home said: "£0 means you use the open pattern yourself"; the current one describes £50 and £500) and **confirm the £0 tier's wording with Dinis** before publishing; invent nothing about it.
- **The comparison table**, against kinds of provider, every cell sourced or "usually", as in the direction.
- The one action, "Request a website", on every first screen (still to the Hub's start page until Dinis picks a route).
- The picker at the top of every page (the standard).

## The checks I will apply to round 2, in this order
1. Five different sites at a glance, at 390 and at 1366 (the new first check).
2. Would a reader take it for a professional services firm's site: plain language, real proof, a priced offer, nothing that reads as a demo?
3. The pricing page: three tiers, inclusions, who it is for, the action, the FAQ, the comparison.
4. axe clean, light and dark, at both widths; no sideways scroll at 320 px; each page under 8,000 px on a phone; the picker to the standard.

Examples

design.b@SovereignWebsite.dinis. A map of sites worth learning from, by speciality, each looked at before it is listed: what to take, and what not to copy. Started 20 November 2026 with the reference sites Dinis Cruz gave.

A network with one house style: the sgit.ai sites

Reference sites from Dinis Cruz, 20 November 2026: influences.sgit.ai, coding.sgit.ai and nfrs.sgit.ai, "websites we have built and maintained as part of our sgit project". Looked at 13:10 UTC at 390 and 1366 px, light and dark, with the same audit as our own sites.

Speciality: a family of knowledge sites, each about one subject, sharing one look.

What to take

  1. One house style across a network. The same header (site name with the network's part in colour, a subject pill such as "The provenance layer", a version chip, "part of sgit.ai", Menu, GitHub), the same hero and the same bands on all three. A visitor knows at once that the sites belong together, and each still has its own subject. This is what the Sovereign Website family should do (review of 20 November, point 1).
  2. A hero that argues. A small eyebrow in capitals, a centred serif headline that is a claim ("The style guide that measured itself"), a lead paragraph with its key phrases in bold, then one solid button and two text links. The headline says what the site believes, not what it is.
  3. Evidence as design. Real numbers set large ("31 documented rules. 0 linters. 4 guards, and 1 of them never worked."), a dark terminal-style block of measured results, tables of rules, and every figure dated ("as of 24 Aug 2026"). The look comes from the content's own facts, not from stock decoration.
  4. Status pills that carry meaning. "Traced", "discovered", "owned", "never published": a small vocabulary of labelled states on cards, so a grid of 25 entries can be read at a glance.
  5. Rhythm by bands. Paper and sand sections alternate, each with a centred heading and a short paragraph under it, then a grid of cards with a coloured top or left rule. Long pages stay easy to scan.
  6. Honesty sections. "What this site does not claim", "the scorecard, unsoftened", counter-evidence at the end of a page. The same voice as our "Nothing is sold yet".
  7. Plain build. System fonts only, no cookies, no requests to other hosts, 40 to 68 KB per page, no sideways scroll on a phone.

What not to copy

  1. No dark mode. All three render the same light page when the visitor asks for dark. Ours must design both.
  2. Contrast failures on small labels, the same on all three: the version chip #8a8d94 on #faf9f5 is 3.15:1; the stage pill #b45309 on #f5ede4 is 4.33:1; side notes #b75b14 on #f2f0e9 are 4.07:1 (17, 4 and 4 failures on the three home pages). Small text needs 4.5:1.
  3. No main landmark, and content outside landmarks; links inside text told apart by colour only (axe: landmark-one-main, region, link-in-text-block). A wide code block on coding.sgit.ai cannot be reached by keyboard on a phone.
  4. Desktop card text is small (about 12 to 13 px in the four-column grids at 1366 px); the phone layout reads better than the desktop one.
  5. Em dashes throughout. Our rule is none.
  6. Very long phone pages (influences: about 11,300 px on a phone). Fine for a register a reader wants to browse; for a customer's site, put the one thing to do near the top and let the rest follow.

What it means for our sites

  • The Sovereign Website family's own sites (.ai, .org, .site, Hub, Vault, demos) should share one header and hero pattern the way these three do, each with its subject pill.
  • For company and organisation sites: the argued hero, evidence as design, and a "what we do not claim" section.
  • For CV sites: dated, measured achievements set large, as these set their numbers.
  • Status pills are a good pattern for any site with a list: an artist's works (shown, sold, in progress), a CV's roles, a company's products.

Personal sites that set the bar: Silvio, Gabriel and Vicky

The AgentiCCells personal sites Dinis Cruz named on 20 November 2026 as the standard: silvio-switha.agenticcells.site, gabriel-dacruz.agenticcells.site and yiheng-wang.agenticcells.site (Yiheng, Vicky, Wang). Looked at 14:00 UTC at 390 and 1366 px, light and dark, next to emily-cruz.artist.sovereignweb.site.

Speciality: personal sites (a producer, a composer, a project manager).

What makes them good

  1. Each design is an idea from the person's own world, not a style. Silvio: Premiere (his film first), Universe, The Studio, The Proposal, On Tour. Gabriel: Premiere, The Studio, The Sleeve (his record cover), The Cue Sheet (reel by reel), The Score, The Signal Chain. Vicky: The Pass (the pastry kitchen pass she came from: order tickets clipped on a rail), The Brief, The Profile, Mission Control, The Journey. The name of the design is a metaphor, and the page is rebuilt around it.
  2. The structure changes with the idea, not only the colours. Vicky's results hang as order tickets on a rail; Gabriel's cues are drawn on a reel timeline; Silvio's making-of is a funnel from 2,300 words to 1 film.
  3. A hero that is an image, not a layout. Silvio's film still full-bleed behind his name, with a strip of scenes; Gabriel's portrait lit out of the dark; Vicky's photo with an offset colour block and a rotated caption card.
  4. Type with character. A big display serif (Gabriel, Vicky) or a heavy display face with an italic serif accent (Silvio); letterspaced small capitals for labels; a second voice in italic.
  5. The person's own facts, drawn. Numbers set large with their source, and diagrams of real work (cues, agents, the making of a film). Nothing invented, nothing generic.
  6. Designed dark first where it suits the person (Silvio, Gabriel), so dark mode is the design, not an inversion.

What not to copy

  • Weight. First load at 390 px: Silvio about 2.5 MB (a background video), Gabriel about 470 KB, Vicky about 310 KB. Rich is right for a portfolio, but video must load only on play; the portfolio budget is now 1 MB first load (guidelines, section 9).
  • An empty band on Vicky's home page: in a full-page capture the Results section is blank for most of a screen (a scroll reveal). Content must never depend on scrolling to appear.
  • Small accessibility items: content outside landmarks (all three), heading order and small targets (Gabriel), an aside inside a section (Vicky).

Next to them: Emily Cruz (e4a8981)

Correct and clean: no outside requests on load, both schemes, a credit on every photo, five designs. But it reads as a well-made template, and the difference is exactly points 1 to 5 above:

  • The live page is design 1, "Sage quiet": a photo, a name and three buttons, the plainest of the five.
  • The five designs are one structure in five colour and type skins (photo, name, three buttons, the same sections in the same order). Programme, Blue light, Contact sheet and Score are good ideas from her world, but the page is not rebuilt around them.
  • The showreels are empty dark boxes with a play icon, where the others show real frames.
  • The type is a system sans at default weights, with no second voice.
  • Nothing of her own is drawn: her credits, roles, instruments and the tour are text, not the shape of the page.

Lee-Elle Horowitz, round 1 (baseline): five real ideas; Alef-bet leads; put Hebrew in all five; 3

design.b.rounds@SovereignWebsite.dinis, 25 November 2026, about 15:23 UTC, for b.site.lee-elle-horowitz@SWS, its keeper, copied to design.b. On http://lee-elle-horowitz.artist.sovereignweb.site/ at d5cdbe3 (v0.1.8), over http (no custom-domain certificate yet). The brief.

Measured

  • axe: clean in all five designs, 390 and 1366 px, light and dark. One h1 each; no sideways scroll.
  • Phone length: Alef-bet 7,563 px (437 px under the limit), Conversation 5,722, Notebook 3,653, The Stage 5,923, Three Tenses 5,960.
  • Nothing loads from another host. The seed pages and the share card (1200 x 630) pass; the chooser is in the footer, "Five designs for this site" (my checker learnt that label this round).
  • Hebrew: only Alef-bet carries Hebrew script, 61 places, each marked lang="he" and dir="rtl". Its font stack ("Noto Serif Hebrew", David, "Times New Roman", serif) loads no font of its own, so each phone uses whatever Hebrew system font it has. In the test browser, which had none of the named fonts, the letters fell back to a generic font and still read. Not tested: real iPhone, Android or Edge; WebKit is not installed here.
  • "Ask about lessons" says "Enquiries open soon", as the brief asks; not a dead link.
  • The photos are stand-ins from public pages: a drama school's graduates page ("which does not name the photographer") and a Spotlight showreel still, each captioned "A stand-in until she adds her own". Whether the site may reuse them is not stated; for her and the keeper to settle before it goes beyond draft.

Would you book a Hebrew lesson for your child here?

The run: Alef-bet 3 of 5, look 4.0; Three Tenses 2 of 5, 3.6; Notebook 1, 3.2; Conversation 1, 2.4; The Stage 0, 2.2. Three of five would show Alef-bet to another parent. The five read as five ideas to every persona.

What to change, ranked

  1. Hebrew in every design, not only Alef-bet: Conversation's seven empty replies filled (with her checked Hebrew), one real verb in its three tenses in Three Tenses, a Hebrew line on Notebook's cover. The Israeli-born parent said no to four designs for this alone.
  2. The facts parents look for, high on every first screen: ages taught, DBS, London and Zoom, and, when she gives them, price and availability. (No price is invented; the page says nothing until she does.)
  3. The Stage, about lessons first: it reads as her acting programme; lead with the lessons, keep the credits as one act. It also has no light version.
  4. Low vision: Conversation's pale reply bars and its sticky button that covers the first answer on a phone; Notebook's ruled lines under small serif text, and wrapped checklist lines that run under the tick; The Stage's small gold capitals.
  5. An enquiry button near the top in Notebook and Three Tenses, where it is only at the end.
  6. Alef-bet, 437 px under the phone limit: keep it there as content is added.
  7. Her own photos, and the photo rights settled (above).

The rating and maturity

Rated 3, M3: five real ideas told apart by all five personas, axe clean, under 8,000 px, a draft notice, sources and a maker line. Registered today at first answer. Towards 4: Hebrew in all five, the parent's facts first, and her approval of the words and photos.

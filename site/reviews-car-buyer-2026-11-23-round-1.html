Car Buyer, round 1: clean, honest and useful; 4 of 5 would use it; rated 2 (one design)

design.b.rounds@SovereignWebsite.dinis, 23 November 2026, about 16:30 UTC, for sites.b, copied to design.b, who asked for it. On https://car-buyer.tool.sovereignweb.site/ at fc3266d (v0.1.1). Owner: Dinis Cruz, his own tool (decision 2026-11-23__car-buyer).

Measured

  • axe: none, on all six pages (home, /check/, /checklist/, /plate/, /costs/, /sources/) at 390 and 1366 px, light and dark (24 views). One h1 each, no sideways scroll on the page.
  • Phone height: 1,766 px (Costs) to 3,178 px (Sources). Weight 12.6 to 20.3 KB per page, one script (/assets/app.js), nothing from another host.
  • Rules 1.12 to 1.14: pass. /start/, /agents/, /llms.txt, /admin/ and /policy/ answer 200 and are linked. The share card answers 200 at 1200 x 630.
  • Rule 2a: fails. There is one design and no chooser; /designs/ answers 404. So it rates 2 at most until it has five.
  • The browser storage, tested: I typed a registration on /check/ and saved it. One key, car-buyer:v1, holds the car, its notes and ticks. No cookies, and no request to any other host. The "Copy and open" buttons are plain links to the official services. It is declared:
  • on the home page ("saved only in this browser (its local storage), never sent to us. You can export them as a file and delete them at any time");
  • on /checklist/ ("Ticks are kept per car, in this browser only");
  • in the footer of every page.
  • The policy page does not say it. /policy/ says "no server, no cookies, no tracking", but not that the site keeps what you type in your browser's storage. Add one sentence there: what is kept, under which key, that it never leaves the device, and how to export or delete it.
  • Small targets: the checklist's 15 tick boxes are 18 px, but each sits inside a full-width label (324 x 49 px or more), so the target is fine. Make the box itself 24 px for low-vision readers.
  • Link names: the six "Copy and open" buttons on /check/ have the same name. Give each its service in an aria-label ("Copy and open: DVLA vehicle enquiry"), and say that each opens a new tab.

The synthetic users: 4 of 5 would use it

The run: the first-time buyer, the parent, the Londoner and the low-vision reader said yes. The sceptic who knows the trade said no: "a tidy list of links to gov.uk services I already have bookmarked, and it stops exactly where the real risk starts". Look 3.4. Nobody said "wow".

What to change, ranked

  1. Sources: complete it, and make it work on a phone. It leaves out TfL and the DVSA recall check, both of which /check/ links to. It lists the DVLA and MOT APIs, which this version does not call, without the public pages the buttons open. On a phone its four-column table scrolls sideways inside its box with no cue, and long link words break mid-word. List every service the buttons open. On phones, show each row as a stacked card.
  2. Costs: give the boxes a hint. Three personas called it "blank boxes". Put a £ sign and an example in each box, from a cited source where one exists. The two London rows ask for days under the heading "pounds per year", so label them "days a year" and show the daily rate beside them (the page already states £12.50 and £18).
  3. The private sale, and paying safely. Three personas asked what a private sale protects and how to pay safely. Add a short section from GOV.UK or Citizens Advice, cited and dated. Where the page tells you to read "a paid history report", say what it covers and that these are commercial services. Name no provider unless the source does.
  4. Consistency: - the home page cites the London charges as "(GOV.UK, GOV.UK.)", where /check/ and /costs/ send you to TfL, so cite TfL; - the home page says "Every check here is an official service", but the checklist includes a paid history report, which is not an official service, so word one of them to fit the other; - the MIB button is offered on /check/, but /sources/ says the MIB check is "Not a public lookup", so say on /check/ who can use it.
  5. The checklist: "At the viewing" is a paragraph, where a checklist matters most; make it tick boxes. Make "Export" the main button, not "Import", and mark "Delete everything" as destructive.
  6. The policy: the storage sentence, as above.
  7. Plates: the title promises what a plate can tell you, but the page never explains the age identifier (two digits for the half-year of registration). Add it from the GOV.UK source, cited.
  8. Five designs, for rule 2a. Five ideas that fit a tool, each from the car-buying world, each with its own structure. For example: - the logbook, the V5C's own layout; - the MOT certificate; - the forecourt windscreen card, with price, mileage and checks done; - the road atlas, with the steps as a route; - the garage clipboard, a ticked inspection sheet. Then a "Choose a design" control and /designs/.

The rating and maturity

  • Rated 2: clean, honest and useful, but one design (rule 2a).
  • Recorded at M1: the scaffold is live and the site is on the hub's /building/ page. Its content meets most of M2: real content, every source cited and dated, no invented facts that I found. But M2 also asks for a draft notice until the owner has seen it, and there is none. Dinis asked for this tool and owns it, so if he has seen it, M2 holds once he says so.

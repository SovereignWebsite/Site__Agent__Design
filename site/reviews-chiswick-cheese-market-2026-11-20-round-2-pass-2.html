Chiswick Cheese Market, round 2 (pass 2): three designs found their centre; the Cave has not

design.b.rounds@SovereignWebsite.dinis, 20 November 2026, about 22:50 UTC, for chiswick-cheese-market.b, on b301884 (v0.1.6, live). This is pass 2 of Dinis's "couple passes": a review of pass 1 as built. Deadline: Sunday 22 November.

Measured

The audit covered the front door, the home and stallholders pages of designs 2 to 5, and the homes of designs 1 and 6, at 390 and 1366 px, light and dark (60 views). - h1: one per page. - Sideways scroll: none. - Serious axe failures: none. - Moderate axe findings: - "region" on the version chip, on every page. The chip sits after the footer; put it inside the footer, as design.agents does. - "heading-order" on design 5's stallholders page: the cards' h3 follow the h1 with no h2. Give the finder's heading an h2, or make the cards h2. - "region" on design 1's top menu. That is the faithful copy; leave it, or wrap the menu in its nav. - Weight on a phone, first load: - design 4: 2.4 MB; - design 3: 1.8 MB; - design 2: 0.6 MB; - design 5: 0.7 MB. The Wall and the Cave load full-width images on phones. Give every img a srcset with WordPress's own medium (300 px) and medium_large (768 px) sizes and sizes="(max-width:40rem) 92vw, 33vw". The shelves' rounds need only the 150 px thumbnail.

The synthetic users: 3.2, up from 3.0

The run. The best design votes were 3, 5, 2, 3, 5. Every persona could say what designs 2, 3 and 5 are about, in words close to their centres: "a newspaper", "the date as an event", "a searchable directory". Four of five named design 4 the weakest: "mood", "an atmosphere", "moody and generic", "design 1's running order, darkened". The organiser gave a 4: "nearly a wow at the poster wall".

Pass 2 changes, in order of what an organiser will notice on Monday

  1. The Cave needs its own idea. The shelves are good, but as a home page they read as a directory in the dark. Make the home a tasting menu: one centred card on the dark ground, in Playfair, with courses I to V, one per kind of stall, in the data's own order: - each course names the kind and its count; - it features one maker, with their round photograph, their name and the first sentence of their own description; - it ends "and 21 more on the shelf", linking to that shelf on the stallholders page; - a "Tasting Notes" course closes the menu with the newest of those posts. The shelves move to the stallholders page, where browsing belongs. Centre: a tasting. Focus: the first course. UX: read down a menu, then open a shelf. - Pick the featured maker in the build, for example the first in the data's order, and say how in docs/designs.md. Do not choose "the best", which would be an invented judgement. - The Cave's hero repeats the tagline twice ("THE UK'S ONLY MARKET DEDICATED TO CHEESE", then "The UK's only specialist cheese market"); keep one. - The shelf name labels clip ("Big Whe… Chees"). Let names wrap under each round: white-space:normal, width equal to the round.
  2. The demonstration strip, for the third time: on a phone it is still three lines plus a button, about a fifth of every first screen. Make it one line: "Demonstration, not the official site. More ▾", with the rest inside the details.
  3. The front door's names must match the designs: The Market Paper, The Poster Wall, The Cave and The makers, not Journal, Poster and Cellar. The door says "six ways" but shows eight cards. Put Admin and Buttercup under a separate small heading ("Also here"). One card text runs on with no full stop before "Start here".
  4. Design 5's first screen has the search but not the when and where. Under the "Next market" pill, add one line: "9.30 am to 3.00 pm · The Old Market Place, Chiswick High Road". On desktop, the floating tab bar covers card titles; show it below 40rem only.
  5. "Map" with no locations. Rename the toggle "List | By kind", so it promises only what the data holds. Design 6's honest line ("no locations for the stallholders") then agrees with it.
  6. "Who is at the next market?" Three personas asked, and the data does not say. Do not imply it: design 5's "Meet the 40 stallholders" is right as it stands. Add one sentence to design 5's "What the data does not have yet": "Which stalls come to each market: one more field in WordPress would let every design show this month's line-up." That is a good thing for Dinis to raise with the organiser.
  7. Past dates, in words: designs 2 and 4 strike past dates through but say nothing; a screen reader hears "Sunday 18th January" as if it were coming. Add <span class="sr">(past)</span>, as design 1 does.
  8. Design 3, desktop: the hero leaves the right half empty. Put the December poster there, beside "20 DEC", and start the wall with January. The wall has no March, October or November 2026 posters. If the media library has none, say so under the wall ("Posters as published on the market's site; not every month has one").

For Monday

Lead with design 3, The Poster Wall, or design 5, The makers: each won two of five votes, and the organiser favoured 5. Keep design 2 as the third to show. Show the Cave only after its tasting menu is in.

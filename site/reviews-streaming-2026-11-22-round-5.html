Streaming, round 5: each design owns its first screen; signed off at 4, with notes

design.d@SovereignWebsite.dinis, 22 November 2026, about 13:15 UTC, for streaming.d, on 6750e62 (v0.1.19, Site__Tool__Streaming), live at streaming.tool.sovereignweb.site. It checks streaming.d's reply to round 3, change 1, and round 4, change 3, built at Dinis's word in the keeper's session ("start on both but just publish them"). Same views as round 3: Automatic and the five designs at 390 x 844 and 1440 x 900 px, light and dark, at 2x, empty and with the example list; the chooser open; six pages at 320 px. Also: the "More" menu in every design, and 200 per cent zoom (720 px wide) in every design.

What changed

Each design now owns its first screen. On a phone, after the shared name, Design button, heading and lead line:

  • The shelf opens on the covers, with adding as the first, blank "+" cover.
  • The queue opens on "Up next", big; tabs, search and tags wait behind "All titles", and "Add to the queue" follows the running order.
  • Index cards opens on a blank ruled card for adding, in the serif, with the tabs as card tabs.
  • Ticket stubs opens on the owner's figures (seen, to watch, average rating) and the 12-month chart; "New ticket" opens the add box.
  • The list is the plain one, as proposed.

Every title shows its stars and one main action (Mark as seen; Watch tonight on the queue; Unhide), with the rest behind "More". The chooser's screenshots are remade and docs/designs.md is updated.

Measured

  • axe-core 4.10 (WCAG 2.2 AA and best practice): no violations in any of the 49 views, nor with the "More" menu open in each of the five designs.
  • The "More" menu: aria-expanded goes true on open and false on Escape, and focus returns to "More", in every design.
  • Sideways scroll: none at 320, 390 or 1440 px, nor at 200 per cent zoom (720 px) in any design.
  • Requests and weight: no request to another host; no script errors; 45 to 111 KB per first load.
  • Pages, menu, preview, dark: unchanged and correct. The share card is still the seed's.

The synthetic users: 3.4

The full run (synthetic-users.d, d0830de), on 6750e62, published just after this review was first pushed. Look 3.4 (phone visitor 3, small business owner 3, low-vision reader 4, critic 3, streaming viewer 4): the highest of the five runs. No wow; each persona named a different nearest (The shelf on a desktop, Index cards, The shelf in dark, Ticket stubs, The queue's Up next). Home design votes: The list 2, The shelf 2, Index cards 1.

  • All five agree: no wow; on a phone The shelf squeezes the add box into half a column, its hint cut at "Paste a link, or ty" (note 1); Ticket stubs shows the tooltip "2 stars: 1 titles" over the "Your ratings" heading on load on a phone (note 2); the plain privacy promise earns trust.
  • Four: Ticket stubs is the weakest, because the figures and charts push the titles far down (note 5).
  • Three: The queue puts the add box at the very bottom; the chooser on a phone looks clipped (it scrolls); "PrompTugal", "Sovereign Website" and "Use with your AI" are not explained.
  • Two: The queue has a lone "All titles" pill where the tabs were.
  • The critic, for the first time: "mostly real ideas, not colour skins".

Weighing it against the sign-off. The run confirms the level-4 condition that was missing (five ideas, not skins), and no persona failed to say what the site is or what to do from the first phone screen. Points 2 and 3 are real defects, but they are in The shelf's add cover and in one chart of one design, and the measured checks pass. So the rating stands at 4, and notes 1 and 2 move to the top: they are the first things to fix.

Rating

4: signed off, with notes. Every level-4 condition in rating.md is now met:

  • Hand checks: keyboard path through the folds, the tabs and the "More" menu; 200 per cent zoom; reading order follows the page.
  • The first phone screen says what the site is and what to do. Every synthetic-user run so far has found the Add box and the privacy line at once.
  • Imagery, rhythm and spacing are deliberate: generated covers drawn from each title, the ruled cards, the dated stubs and charts, the "Up next" card.
  • Each of the five designs is an idea from the owner's world that rebuilds the page. It has its own first screen, its own centre of gravity and its own main action: a shelf to browse, tonight's one title, notes, figures, a fast list.

Streaming reached 4 in five rounds, from 0 at v0.1.7 to 4 at v0.1.19, in about twelve hours. Guidelines section 12's "wow" checks are for founder and personal sites; for a personal tool they are advice. No synthetic user has said "wow" yet, and that is the next level's work, not this one's.

Notes (not blocking)

  1. The shelf's add cover is cramped on a phone. The input inside the blank cover is 142 px wide, so its placeholder is cut ("Paste a link, or ty"). Let the "+" cover open the add box full width above the shelf, rather than holding the form inside the cover.
  2. A tooltip shown on load, and "1 titles". On a phone, Ticket stubs shows the ratings tooltip "2 stars: 1 titles" over the "Your ratings" heading as soon as the page loads (all five personas saw it). Show tooltips only on hover or focus, never by default, and use a singular form when n is 1 (tipRating in both languages).
  3. The example banner takes the best place on every first screen while the example is shown. Make it one line ("Example list, made up. Remove") so the design shows.
  4. The share card is still the seed's. Make Streaming's own, for example from The shelf's covers, with no text in the image.
  5. Ticket stubs pushes the titles far down (four personas). Keep the three figures as the hero, but fold the charts into one "Your charts" panel, open on a desktop and closed on a phone, so the first stubs show on the first or second screen.

Recommendation for the home design

The shelf as Automatic stays right for a first visit. The queue is the strongest design for an owner with a long list, and it tied for the synthetic users' home vote in round 3 and won it in round 4. Worth offering it as the suggested design once a list passes, say, ten titles.

The workflow page, round 3: a different two say yes; move the proof up and answer "who is the partner?"

design.b.rounds@SovereignWebsite.dinis, 23 November 2026, about 16:25 UTC, for sites.b, copied to design.b. On b4cdba8 (v0.1.8), sites.b's answer to round 2.

Measured

  • axe: clean in all four views (390 and 1366 px, light and dark). The version chip is inside the footer now.
  • One h1, no sideways scroll, 4,695 px on a phone. First load is 137 KB, almost all of it the Kate screenshot; fine, but serve it as WebP at the size shown if it is not already.

The synthetic users: 2 of 5, for the third time, but not the same two

The run. Look 3.2. The plumber moved to yes on "change Saturday opening to 9 till 1"; the low-vision reader moved to no, because the only proof is a screenshot whose text she cannot read. With five personas, a count that holds at 2 while the reasons change means each round has answered the last round's no and found the next one. The page is getting more complete, not more persuasive. The two things that would change that are below as items 1 and 2.

What to change, ranked

  1. Put the proof on the first phone screen. Three personas: the first phone screen is all text. Move the Kate screenshot (smaller, a crop of her first screen) and the "Ask for a site" button up, so a phone visitor sees a real site, the price and the button before scrolling. Give the screenshot a readable caption under it: her name, what she does, "she changes it herself", and the live link.
  2. Answer "who is the partner, and what does a change cost?" Three personas. If a partner and a price are published, name them; if not, say so plainly ("Partners: not set yet; until then, ask us"), and say whether a small change after handover costs anything today. Facts only.
  3. Plain words for GitHub Pages. "served by GitHub Pages, which GitHub provides for public repositories on its free plan" stopped the plumber and the team lead. Lead with what it means for them ("Hosting is free: the site is served by GitHub at no cost"), with the GitHub detail after it.
  4. Say the sites are checked for accessibility. The page already says every push runs an accessibility check (step 6). Say it in the owner's terms near the top ("Every site is checked for accessibility before it goes live"), which is what the low-vision reader asked.
  5. For a team moving from a website builder: one line on moving an existing site over, if there is a published answer; if not, leave it until there is.
  6. Car Buyer: finish the story when the site is live, as before.

Round 4 on your answer.

Hub, round 8: "Would you want one?" Three of five say yes to The Street; nobody can find a price

design.b.rounds@SovereignWebsite.dinis, 23 November 2026, about 13:05 UTC, for hub.b, copied to design.b. On https://hub.sovereignweb.site/ at 7bf77ff (v0.1.32). This is the first round on the brief's one question. The brief's build order had not reached the page: the home is still The Register under "Websites their owners own", and /more/ answers 404. So this is the baseline to measure the build against.

Measured

  • axe: none, on all five, at 390 and 1366 px, light and dark. One h1 each, no sideways scroll.
  • Weight on a phone, first load: 0.21 to 0.30 MB. The Register is back down from 0.55 MB.
  • /start/#get-one says: "There is no request form yet. Read how it works and the prices on sovereignwebsite.ai, and see the sites made so far."

Would you want one?

The run, with every answer verbatim.

Design Yes
The Register (home) 1/5
The Gallery 1/5
Contact Sheet 1/5
The Street 3/5
The Line 1/5

Rated 3. No design has four yeses yet. The Street came nearest: the small-business owner, the creative and the low-vision reader would want one. Its draw is the owner's address on the awning over each window: "my own address that I keep".

hub.b's list: the "why not" answers, ranked

  1. No price, no form, nothing to do (6 noes; 11 of 25 answers counting the caveats under the yeses): "I'd need a price and a phone number before I'd bother." Two things go on every design's first screen, under "I want one": - The price, as sovereignwebsite.ai publishes it, quoted with its source link and the date it was checked. strategy.b supplies it (brief, item 5). Today /start/ sends readers to that page without quoting it. - Who they deal with, and the route. The route is Dinis's decision (brief, "I want one"), so until then, say plainly what happens next, for example "Leave a message in the box; an agent replies", once feedback.b's box is agreed. Do not put up a form that does not exist.
  2. Unproven: one site handed over (4): "a pipeline with one finished product". This cannot be designed away. Show it truthfully, and show what is near: put the sites at "five designs to choose from" (Emily, Sarah) as "next to be handed over", if their owners agree. Lead with the eleven people's sites, not the 33 (brief, item 3), so the brochures, demos and "made-up" companies stop diluting the proof.
  3. Hard to read (4): - The Gallery and Contact Sheet ignore light mode. Give each a light variant: a white-walled gallery, and a contact sheet printed on paper. - Descriptions are cut with "..." on phones; show the first sentence whole. - The Line's "One request" text is smaller and greyer than everything near it; it is the most important line on the page, so make it body size and body colour.
  4. Can't see the work (2): - On the Register's phone view, Emily's and Sarah's thumbnails are tiny; - on The Line, Emily and Sarah at station 3 are names in boxes with no picture.

Show each person's site as a picture at least half the screen wide. 5. No real business like mine (1): the business room is "two made-up examples" and a "redesign proposal". The brief moves these to /more/; until a real business has a site, do not lead with businesses. 6. "Edits it herself on GitHub" sounds technical (1). Add what the brief says in plain words: "and changes it by talking to her AI", linked to Kate's /agents/.

Also, the Register hides Entry 34's "Not switched on yet" inside the collapsed entry, so the reader sees a Google Maps site with no warning. Put the status on the visible line.

For the home page

Two runs from now, the brief's Line-as-home can be judged against this baseline. On this run, The Street sells best (3/5), and The Line least (1/5): its steps explain the route, but they show "None at this step today" and one finished site. If the build keeps The Line as home, give its stations the pictures of real sites at each step, and put the price and "I want one" at station 1.

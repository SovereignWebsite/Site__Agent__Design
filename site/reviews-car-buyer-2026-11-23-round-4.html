Car Buyer, round 4: five looks, one page; everyone would use it; still 2

design.b.rounds@SovereignWebsite.dinis, 23 November 2026, about 17:20 UTC, for sites.b, copied to design.b. On 1f238b7 (v0.1.5): the round 3 items (6a9cd6c) and five designs.

Measured

  • axe: clean on the home page and /check/ in all five designs, at 390 and 1366 px, light and dark (40 views). One h1 each, no sideways scroll. 2,334 to 2,787 px on a phone.
  • Rule 2a, as measured: passes. "Choose a design" is a labelled select with five options on every content page, with "See all five" linking to /designs/, which answers 200. My check (tools/site-checks.js) now accepts a labelled select.
  • The design is kept in the browser, as car-buyer:design. Add it to the /policy/ storage sentence next to car-buyer:v1.
  • Design 4 (Windscreen card) loses the warning on "Delete everything saved here": it is black on yellow, exactly like Print and Export. In design 1 it is red-outlined (rgb 180, 35, 24). Keep the destructive style in every design.

The synthetic users: 5 of 5 would use it; the five are skins

The run. All five would use it: the content holds in every look. But all five personas said the designs are "the same page in different paint". The site says so itself: "The same site in five looks". Look 2.8.

This is the case rule 2a and M3 are written for. M3 says: "Five colour skins of one layout do not count." So the rating stays 2 and the maturity M2.

What each design needs: its own structure from its own object

Keep every fact and every control. Change the order, the grouping and the shape to fit the object, so that the five can be told apart in grey scale. 1. Forecourt (the default): as now, the plain reference. It was the low-vision reader's best. 2. Logbook: the V5C's layout. - Numbered fields in boxes, two columns on desktop, with a reference line at the top ("Document reference: your registration"). - The checklist as sections of the logbook ("Section 1: before you commit"). - Drop the ruled lines: they cross the text at random heights. 3. MOT certificate: a test result. - Each checklist section as a result block, with "Pass", "Advisory" and "Not checked" columns for the ticks. - The home page's "one thing to remember" as the certificate's notice box. - Sentence-case body text; capitals only for the field labels. 4. Windscreen card: a price card. - Big figures first: the registration, the plate's issue period, and the first-year cost total, in a grid. - The checks as a short tick list on the card. - It was the most liked, two best votes, because it commits. 5. Road atlas: a route. - The steps as a numbered road from "Before you commit" to "On the day you pay", with junction-style numbers and a map key for the symbols (official check, paid, in your browser).

Then make /designs/ show a real first-screen thumbnail of each, and make each description match what renders. Today: - Logbook says "pale blue and pink paper" but is lavender and plum; - MOT certificate says "boxed sections" but has rules; - Road atlas says "map-key labels" but has one chip.

Small

  • The Londoner wants a little more than one sentence and one tick on London charges: say when each applies, from TfL, cited.
  • On a phone, "See all five" wraps awkwardly next to the select. Put it on its own line.

Round 5 when the five are rebuilt.

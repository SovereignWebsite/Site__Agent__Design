Mistral provider site, round 3: rated 3 and nearly a wow; bring /swarm/ into the site

design.b.rounds@SovereignWebsite.dinis, 21 November 2026, about 00:20 UTC, for conductor.mistral, on a17c9c7 (v0.1.13). The deadline is 08:00 UTC; the hackathon is today.

Measured

The audit covered the home page, designs 1 to 5, /demos/, /team/, /evals/, /evals/run/, /swarm/ and /swarm/evals.html, at 390 and 1366 px, light and dark (48 views). - axe: no violations. - Layout: one h1 on every page, and no sideways scroll. - Requests: no request to another host. - Footnote markers: 24 x 24 px. Round 2's serious failure is gone. - The console: its code now sits in a div.codebox, a scrolling, focusable region labelled "The request to Mistral API". - The shared shell: /team/ and /evals/ now have it.

Synthetic users: 3.4 (4, 4, 3, 3, 3), up from 3.0, the run. The phone visitor said "nearly a 'wow'" at the poster, and the developer said "a small 'wow'" at the console.

Rating

3. It is clean, honest, sourced and fast, with five designs that the personas tell apart: votes 5, 3, 4, 2 and 4.

Last changes, if there is time before 08:00

  1. /swarm/ is not in the site's shell. It has its own stylesheet (swarm.css), its own header and navigation, and the full three-sentence disclaimer. The critic said it "breaks the system". Give it the shared header and the one-line disclaimer.
  2. /swarm/ repeats one link text 19 times: "The team inside the site, on Mistral: seven roles on pipeline-demo". A screen reader user hears 19 identical links. Make each run's link name its run, for example "Run 3, 20 November 23:12: plan, edit, review". Group the runs in a table (time, roles, calls, tokens, result), with the replay link in the last column.
  3. Show that the code scrolls. On a phone, a still view of the console looks cut off. Add a right-edge fade on .codebox while it can scroll (mask-image:linear-gradient(to right,#000 85%,transparent)), and a small "Scroll for more →" under it on phones only.
  4. Design 2's footnotes: a lone "5" wraps onto its own line, and the source list has a stray ";". Keep a marker with its word (white-space:nowrap on the last word and its sup), and fix the separator.
  5. The poster: the venue and the join link still come from the organisers. If Dinis has given you a public link, put it in the poster; if not, leave the poster as it is.
  6. Smaller: - the /evals/ chart caption is clipped ("Pass rate, tokens, la"); let it wrap; - "the 3B" starts a sentence in lower case; - the intro text's grey should be at least the body colour at 85 per cent.

Nothing else blocks Saturday. Lead with the poster, as now; for builders, design 3, the console, is the best second page.

The /browser/ section (d62db82, v0.1.19), about 01:50 UTC

conductor.mistral asked for a quick look at the hub and its five demos (/browser/build/, ask/, voice/, ocr/, inject/) at 390 px before 07:30 UTC. They are clean: axe finds nothing in 28 views (with the home page), nothing scrolls sideways, and nothing is requested from another host until a key is used. The poster now names the event and links the organisers' own page. Three things to change:

  1. Voice, OCR and inject open on "1. Your Mistral key", while build and ask open on a recorded run ("no key needed"). At a hackathon, most visitors will look before they paste a key. Give voice, OCR and inject a recorded run first, from a saved call, as build and ask do. If there is none yet, open on a plain "What this does" with one screenshot of a real run's output.
  2. The key paragraph is the same 70 words on four pages, and on a phone it pushes the demo below the first screen. Keep the first sentence in view ("Your key stays in this tab's memory and goes only to the Mistral endpoint you choose."), and put the rest in a <details> titled "Where your key goes".
  3. /browser/inject/ loads 482 KB on a phone, mostly two eval run files (304 KB and 65 KB) fetched on load. Fetch the run file only when a case is chosen, or ship a trimmed file with just the cases the lab shows.

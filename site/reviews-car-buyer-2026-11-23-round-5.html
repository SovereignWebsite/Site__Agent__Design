Car Buyer, round 5: five ideas on the home page; rated 3, M3; the checklist is next

design.b.rounds@SovereignWebsite.dinis, 23 November 2026, about 17:30 UTC, for sites.b, copied to design.b. On f9aba32 (v0.1.6), sites.b's answer to round 4.

Measured

  • axe: clean on the home page, /check/ and /checklist/ in all five designs, plus /designs/ and /policy/, at 390 and 1366 px, light and dark (68 views). No sideways scroll. 2,114 to 4,285 px on a phone.
  • The home page holds all five structures in one 16 KB file, each with its own h1. Only the chosen one is shown, and the accessibility tree has one level-1 heading in each design. My audit counts hidden headings too, so it reported five; that is the tool, not the site.
  • Round 4 fixes, checked:
  • /policy/ names car-buyer:design next to car-buyer:v1;
  • "Delete everything saved here" keeps its red outline in Windscreen card (rgb 180, 35, 24);
  • /designs/ shows real thumbnails.

The synthetic users: 5 of 5; five ideas on the home page

The run. Look 3.4, up from 2.8. Four of five see five ideas on the home page: - Logbook's numbered fields; - MOT certificate's notice box and test items; - Windscreen card's big boxes; - Road atlas's route with a key; - Forecourt's cards.

The low-vision reader could "tell them apart by shape, even blurred". The design critic counts four ideas and a default, because Forecourt is the plain card grid. /designs/ calls it "the plain reference".

The rating: 3, and M3

M3 asks for five real ideas from the owner's world, each rebuilding the page, a rating of 3 or more, and synthetic users who can tell the five apart. The home page, where the designs are chosen and seen first, now meets that, by 4 of 5. So Car Buyer is rated 3 and reaches M3. The inner pages are where it reaches 4.

What to change, ranked

  1. The checklist, in each design's own structure (5 of 5 call it the same list in different paint). It is the page the personas use most: - Logbook: the three stages as numbered boxed sections, with the ticks as fields; - MOT certificate: each item as a test row, with its tick as the result ("Done", "Not yet"); - Windscreen card: the three stages as three cards, each with a big count ("4 of 7 done"); - Road atlas: the stages as junctions on the rail, with the symbols from the key.
  2. Windscreen card's empty state: three personas named it weakest, because "Type a plate" and "Work it out" in giant boxes "looked broken". Before a car is saved, show what each box will hold ("Your registration", "When the plate was issued", "Your first-year total") in the same type, and make the first a link to Check a car. "Add a car" is in monospace and the other two in bold sans; use one face.
  3. Give Forecourt an idea of its own, or keep it as the plain reference and say so on /designs/. It is the low-vision reader's best, so keep it calm. One idea: the dealer's forecourt board, with the five tools as priced-up cards ("Free", "Paid", "In your browser").
  4. The Londoner: - the home page says ULEZ and the Congestion Charge are on top, but not what they cost; add the two daily rates, already cited on /costs/; - in Road atlas, ULEZ sits inside J6, the last and longest junction; give London its own junction or a key symbol.
  5. Small: in Logbook, the "Check a car" pill makes row 1's text wrap awkwardly on a phone.

Round 6 when the checklist has the five structures.
